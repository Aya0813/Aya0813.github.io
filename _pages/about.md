### Hard Skills

**What kinds of models have I worked with?**

- Mostly LLMs and multimodal LLMs, including text-based and image-text models, as well as CNNs for image modalities.

**What did I do with these models?**

Depending on the problem and model size, I have trained models from scratch and post-trained existing models using Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT) and Reinforcement Learning Fine-Tuning (RLFT). I have also worked on model compression and optimization, including quantization.

**What data did I use?**

- When suitable data already exists, I work on processing, cleaning, and preparing it for training and evaluation, while paying attention to how well different populations are represented; particularly when working with electronic health record (EHR) data.
- When the required data does not exist, I have also built retrieval, embedding, and synthetic-data generation pipelines to create it.

**How did I evaluate the models I trained?**

- After training a model (while following the usual good practices around cross-validation, avoiding data leakage, and monitoring overfitting), I typically move to Weights & Biases (W&B) to track how the model is actually behaving.
- From there, I look at the validation curves and metrics to decide what needs attention next: a particular hyperparameter, more or better data, a different training setup, or simply more experimentation.
- This is also where being comfortable with GPU and HPC workflows has been especially useful, since evaluating different configurations often means running many experiments efficiently. Git and Linux are naturally part of that workflow as well, helping me keep experiments organized, reproducible, and easier to compare.

**Did I ever make these models leave my laptop? (Edge deployment)**

Yes! I have worked on deploying and optimizing deep-learning models on resource-constrained hardware, notably NVIDIA Jetson Xavier. This included techniques such as quantization to significantly reduce model memory requirements while preserving performance.

❗ Of course, there is still a lot to learn. The sections above are simply meant to give a concrete picture of the kinds of technical problems I have worked on so far. **I learn as I go**, and I am always willing to pick up whatever tools, methods, or concepts are needed for the problem at hand.

The _Projects_ and _Publications_ sections of my Website provide the more **concrete evidence** behind these skills: including the code, experiments, and papers.
